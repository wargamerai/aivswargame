      How to play

      Setup and objectiveChoose Egypt, Syria, Iraq, Iran, or Saudi Arabia. The other four countries are controlled by the AI. Pick one of two leader cards. A sole royal candidate takes priority. Your leader provides starting prestige. Each country starts with zero military strength and five action cards. Any event in the opening deal is drawn again. The first country to reach 16 prestige wins.

      Taking a turnUse up to two different actions or card types. Select an action or card beneath the map, then select its target if applicable. You can pass at any time. When you confirm a discard, you spend one action and play moves to the next country. At the start of your own turn, refill your hand to five cards without spending an action. The progress button on the right advances AI actions, responses, and the turn order. Shift-click a card to enlarge it.

      Income and eventsAt the start of your turn, collect $1 per oil field you control. A leader with dollar symbols rolls one die for an additional $1. With one symbol, you need a 6. With two, you need 5–6. With three, you need 4–6. After the opening deal, events resolve immediately. Oil Prices Rise pays every country by its controlled oil fields. Leader Dies affects the country that drew it.

      Actions and alignmentBuy one regular weapon for $2, or spend $1 on aid to the PLO. The aid succeeds on 1–4 for +1 prestige. Choose a neutral, pro-US, or pro-Soviet alignment. Staying neutral throughout the turn earns +1 prestige. The US and Soviet cards import one unit for a matching alignment. France can also import one unit while neutral. At the start of a battle, a matching US or Soviet card can provide three or two reinforcements respectively.

      Diplomacy and revolutionDiplomacy succeeds if the roll is no higher than your military strength. A 6 fails against adjacent countries, and 4–6 fail against nonadjacent countries. Success earns +2 prestige and allies the target if it is a minor state. A Revolution card can annex your allied minor state on 1–4 for +4 prestige.

      Combat and IsraelOpposing units cancel one for one, starting with the fewest stars. Cancelled units with stars return on a die roll no higher than their stars. Regular units use their leader’s stars. Foreign military units normally have one star. Repeat while both sides have units. A successful military attack on Israel gives the attacker +5 prestige, and a failed one gives +3. Eligible countries with a land connection may join, earning +2 on a win or +1 on a loss.

      Coalition deploymentAn attack on a minor state resolves immediately. Before the occupying country's next turn begins, another player can play a United States card on their own turn to declare a coalition for that minor state. Pause the turn for one circuit. US and French cards add three or one strength. The Soviet Union can veto, and the UN Security Council can overturn the veto. If the veto still stands after the circuit, cancel the deployment. A successful coalition attack restores neutrality.

      Leaders and nuclear weaponsOn a leader’s death, compare the reserve card with one newly drawn card. The old leader’s prestige is lost. Prestige from minor-state alliances, annexations, and nuclear weapons survives succession. A rival alliance or annexation removes the prestige earned from that state. France builds a nuclear plant and can convert it on a later turn. An Israel card can destroy a plant before the builder’s next turn. A completed nuclear weapon earns +4 permanent prestige.
